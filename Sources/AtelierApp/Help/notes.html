Notes on photos

Write yourself a sticky note on any photo: what to fix, why you kept it, a list of things to try. Notes float over the photo in Library, Loupe, Develop and Cull, and never change the photo or its zoom.

Show and hide the notes

  • View › Show Notes (⌥⌘N) shows or hides the panel. It stays shown or hidden in every view and after a relaunch.
  • The panel shows the notes of the photo in front: the focused photo in the Grid, the photo in Loupe and Develop, and the focused shot in Cull.
  • Photos with notes carry a small yellow note mark on their thumbnail in the Grid and Filmstrip.
  • Culling writes its own note, by Cull, on each photo it didn't keep, with a bullet for each reason (eyes closed, softer than the keeper…). See Reasons on dropped photos.
  • Move it: drag the panel's top strip; it snaps to the nearest corner. Resize it: drag the grip on the corner facing the middle of the photo. Atelier remembers both.

Write a note

  1. Click + in the panel, or choose Photo › Add Note… (⌥⇧⌘N), or right-click a photo and choose Add Note… The panel opens if it was hidden.
  2. Type. Each note is saved as you type. If a change can't be saved (for example, the catalog's disk is full), a red line under the panel's header says so until a later change saves.
  3. Press Esc or ⌘Return, or click Done, to finish. A note left empty goes away.

Several photos at once: select them in the Grid first. The panel says “Adding to 12 photos”, and the note goes to each of them when you finish.

Change a note: click it. Delete one: point at it and click ⋯ › Delete, or right-click it. It goes at once, with Undo in the panel for ten seconds.

Lists, checkboxes and bold

Notes are plain text with a few marks, as in Notes and many chat apps:

- or *
A bullet. Return starts the next one; Return on an empty bullet ends the list.
1.
A numbered list; Return carries on with the next number.
[]
A checkbox. Click a box in the note to tick it, without opening the note.
Tab, ⇧Tab
Moves a list line in or out (two levels).
⌘B, ⌘I
Bold (**word**) and italic (*word*).
⇧Return
A new line without a bullet.

While you type, the marks stay visible but dimmed, so what you see is what is saved. Once you finish, the note shows real bullets, numbers and checkboxes.

Prefer buttons? While a note is open, the strip above the text has Bullets, Numbered list, Checkboxes, Bold and Italic. A list button turns the selected lines into that list, or back into plain lines. An empty note also shows a hint with the typed shortcuts.

Find photos by their notes

  • The Library search field also searches notes.
  • The filter bar's Notes menu (and the Filmstrip's Filter pop-up) shows only photos with notes, without notes, or with Unticked Checkboxes: your to-do list across the catalog.

Virtual copies, XMP and privacy

  • Each virtual copy has its own notes. A new copy, Edit In version or AI result starts with the original's notes.
  • Notes are saved in the photo's XMP sidecar in Atelier's own field, so they come back if you import the photo into another Atelier catalog. They never go into the Caption or any field other apps show, and Export never includes them.
  • Undo (⌘Z) takes back a note a chat app added.
  • In the Cull view a note can be pinned to a spot on the photo, shown there as a numbered marker. See Note markers.

Notes from chat apps

A connected chat app can read a photo's notes and add one (it is signed with the app's name, such as “Claude”). It cannot change or delete your notes. See Connect a chat app.

Font, color and opacity

In Atelier › Settings… › Appearance › Notes:

Notes font
The handwriting-style Noteworthy (comes with macOS) or the system font.
Color
Sticky-note yellow (the default), pink, green, blue, orange or purple, or any color from the color well. The text turns light on a dark color.
Opacity
85% to start, so the photo shows faintly through the paper; 100% is solid. The text always stays solid. The note is a flat see-through fill, so it never slows the photo down.

Beyond Lightroom. Lightroom Classic has no per-photo notes; people use the Caption field, which is exported with the photo. Atelier's notes are private and stay out of exports.

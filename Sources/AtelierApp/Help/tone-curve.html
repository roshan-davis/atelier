Tone Curve presets

The Preset menu at the top of the panel sets the whole curve at once: the Parametric regions and splits, the RGB, Red, Green and Blue curves and Refine Saturation. Nothing else on the photo changes, so it works on top of any Basic, HSL or Color Grading setting. The menu shows the name of the preset the curve matches, or Custom once you change it.

  1. Preview: open the menu and rest the pointer on a preset. The Navigator (top left) shows the photo with that curve; the photo itself doesn't change until you click.
  2. Apply: click it. It is one step in History (“Tone Curve Preset: Cross Process”), so ⌘Z undoes it.
  3. Save your own: shape the curve, then Preset › Save Preset… and name it. It is listed under User Presets on every photo and catalog. Saving under a name you already used replaces that preset. Delete Preset removes one.

Linear clears the whole curve. Basic has Lightroom's Medium and Strong Contrast points. The rest are looks, most made with the Red, Green and Blue curves:

Film Process
Cross Process (slide film in negative chemicals: yellow highlights, cyan shadows, hard contrast), Reverse Cross Process (negative film as slide: flat, pale, cyan-green), Lomo (toy camera: deep contrast, inky shadows), Expired Film and Instant Film.
Cinematic
Teal & Orange, Matrix Green, Neon Night (magenta-blue) and Day for Night (a daylight shot darkened and turned blue to read as night).
Fade
Matte, Faded Film and Vintage Fade: lifted blacks, softer whites.
Effects
Negative, Color Negative (a negative with film's orange cast) and Solarize (the darkroom Sabattier effect: the brightest tones turn back toward dark).

The ⋯ menu in the Point view still saves and applies a single channel's curve.

Beyond Lightroom. Lightroom Classic's Point Curve menu saves the point curve only. Atelier's presets carry the whole panel, and preview in the Navigator before you click. To save a curve together with other settings, use a Develop preset (Presets) and tick only the parts you want.

Parametric curve

The quick way to shape tones without placing points. Pick Parametric at the top of the panel.

  1. Targeted Adjustment (◎), then Parametric or Point.
  2. The curve over the histogram. Drag up or down on it to move the region under the pointer, which is shaded.
  3. Split points: drag a triangle to move where one region ends and the next begins. Right-click the graph to reset regions or splits.
Highlights
The brightest tones, above the right triangle.
Lights
Between the middle and right triangles.
Darks
Between the left and middle triangles.
Shadows
The darkest tones, below the left triangle.

Each goes from −100 to +100. Black and white never move, so Shadows +100 opens up dark areas without turning black gray. Lights up and Darks down is a gentle S-curve. The region curve works on top of Basic tone, as in Lightroom.

Point curve

  1. Channel: RGB changes brightness; Red, Green and Blue change one color each. The ⋯ menu (also on right-click) has Linear, Medium Contrast and Strong Contrast, your saved curves, Save Curve…, Copy and Paste a channel, Reset, Snap to Grid and Show All Curves.
  2. The curve, over the photo's histogram. The dashed diagonal is “no change”. Left is black, right is white.
  3. Input and Output of the selected point, 0 to 255. Type a number to move it.
  1. Add a point: click anywhere on the curve, then drag it. Up brightens those tones, down darkens them. A curve holds up to 16 points.
  2. Move a point: drag it, or with the pointer over the graph press the arrow keys (⇧ for bigger steps). Points stay in order from left to right.
  3. Remove a point: drag it off the graph, or double-click it. The two end points always stay.

Common shapes

Basic tone, the region curve and the RGB curve keep each color's hue, as Lightroom does: a curve makes colors brighter or darker, never a different color. The faint second line under the RGB curve shows the combined result of Basic tone and the region curve.

Red, Green and Blue curves

These work on one channel at a time, so they do shift color, on purpose. Raise the Blue curve's left end to put blue into the shadows; lower its right end for yellow highlights. Turn on Show All Curves to see the other channels faintly. For tinting by tone, Color Grading is usually easier.

Refine Saturation

Shown under the Point curve once the RGB curve is bent. A steep curve makes colors more saturated as well as more contrasty, as in Lightroom (100, the default). Lower it to keep the contrast but hold back the extra saturation; at 0 the curve changes brightness only.

Targeted Adjustment

Click ◎ at the top of the panel, then drag up or down on the photo over the tone you want to change. In Parametric it moves the region sliders for that tone; in Point it adds a point for that tone on the channel shown (or moves the one already there). Each drag is one step in History. Click ◎ again or press Esc to put it away.

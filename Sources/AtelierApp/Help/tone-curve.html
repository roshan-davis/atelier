Tone Curve

Reshape brightness exactly where you want it. The curve maps each tone of the photo (along the bottom) to a new brightness (up the side).

Edit the curve

  1. Channel: RGB changes brightness; Red, Green and Blue change one color each. ↺ resets the channel shown.
  2. The curve, over the photo's histogram. The dashed diagonal is “no change”. Left is black, right is white.
  3. Region sliders: a quick way to bend the RGB curve without placing points.
  1. Add a point: click anywhere on the curve, then drag it. Up brightens those tones, down darkens them.
  2. Move a point: drag it. Points stay in order from left to right.
  3. Remove a point: double-click it. The two end points always stay.

Common shapes

Basic tone, the region sliders and the RGB curve keep each color's hue, as Lightroom does: a curve makes colors brighter or darker, never a different color. The faint second line under the RGB curve shows the combined result of Basic tone and the region sliders.

Red, Green and Blue curves

These work on one channel at a time, so they do shift color, on purpose. Raise the Blue curve's left end to put blue into the shadows; lower its right end for yellow highlights. For tinting by tone, Color Grading is usually easier.

Region sliders

Highlights
The brightest quarter of tones.
Lights
Bright midtones.
Darks
Dark midtones.
Shadows
The darkest quarter.

Each goes from −100 to +100 and bends the curve smoothly in its range. Lights up and Darks down is a gentle S-curve.

Develop panel

Calibration

Reshapes the red, green and blue the photo's colors are made of. One move shifts every color in the photo at once, so it's the place for a film-like palette or a camera whose colors you want to nudge in every shot.

Red, Green and Blue PrimaryShadows TintWhere it works

Red, Green and Blue Primary

Every color in the photo is a mix of three primaries. Moving one primary moves every color that has some of it in the mix, which is why a small change here reaches further than the same change in HSL / Color. White, grays and black stay neutral whatever you do.

Hue
Turns the primary toward its neighbours. Red moves toward orange (right) or magenta (left), green toward cyan (right) or yellow (left), blue toward purple (right) or cyan (left). The track shows where it goes.
Saturation
Makes everything that has this primary in it richer (right) or more muted (left).

Shadows Tint

Adds green (left) or magenta (right) to the shadows only, keeping their brightness. It's strongest in the deepest shadows and fades out before the midtones, so it can fix a camera that renders blacks a little green or magenta without changing the white balance of the rest of the photo.

Where it works

Calibration comes first among the color and tone panels, right after the profile and white balance, as in Lightroom. So Basic, HSL / Color and Color Grading all work on the reshaped colors, and Color Range masks still pick colors from the photo before it.

  • Turn the panel off with the switch in its header to compare, as with any panel.
  • New presets have a Calibration part, ticked by default; Copy and Paste Settings carry it too.
  • Masks don't have Calibration sliders: it changes the whole photo.

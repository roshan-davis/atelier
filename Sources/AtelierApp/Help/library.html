Imports
One entry per import, named after its folder. Right-click to Rename…, Import More Photos Into This…, Cull… or Delete Import…. Deleting the list keeps the photos in the catalog and on disk, unless you tick Also delete the photos and move their files to the Trash. That box always starts unticked, and ticking it shows how many files will go to the Trash before you confirm with Delete Import and Files.
Volumes
Your drives and the NAS, each with its folders as they are on disk. An offline drive is marked; its photos stay browsable.

Filter and search

The bar above the photos narrows the current list:

  • Search: type part of a filename, title, caption or keyword.
  • Filters: a minimum Rating (★ + to ★★★★★ +), a Flag (Picked, Unflagged, Rejected) and a Label. The button shows how many are on.

Filters apply to whatever is selected in the sidebar, and to the Filmstrip.

Inspector

The right panel in Grid and Loupe (toggle it with the Inspector toolbar button). Its sections open and close like Develop's panels: click a header, or right-click one for Solo Mode, Expand All and Collapse All. Long names end in “…”; hover to read them in full. From the top:

  • Histogram of the photo as edited, with clipping triangles, and under it the ISO, focal length, aperture and shutter speed it was taken with. Hover over that line for the camera, lens and exposure compensation. Editing it is in Develop.
  • File: name, Paired With (the JPEG shot with a RAW, shown as one photo), copy name, folder, drive, dimensions and file size.
  • Camera: capture time, camera, lens, exposure, ISO, focal length, exposure bias, flash, exposure program and metering mode.
  • Rating, flag and label, Keywords (comma separated; Apply Keywords adds them to every selected photo), and Title and caption.

In Loupe the photo itself also shows its name, when it was taken, its size and its camera settings in the corner, as Lightroom's Loupe info does.

Collections and imports

The Organize toolbar menu works on the selected photos: New Collection with Selection…, Add to a collection, Add to Import, and, when you're in a collection or import, Remove from This Collection or Remove from This Import. Removing from a list never removes the photo from the catalog.

Filmstrip

The strip of thumbnails along the bottom, in every view, showing the current list in the same order as the grid. Its header shows the list's name, how many photos, how many are selected, which one you're on, and its filename.

  • Click, ⌘-click and ⇧-click select, as in the grid. Right-click for the photo menu.
  • Drag its top edge to make thumbnails bigger or smaller.
  • Hide or show it with the small tab in the middle of the bottom edge, View › Show Filmstrip, or F6.

XMP sidecars

Ratings, flags, labels, keywords, titles and captions live in the catalog. To let Lightroom, Bridge or Capture One see them, write them to .xmp files next to the originals:

  • Library › Write XMP for Selection (⌘S) or Write All Unsaved XMP (⌥⌘S).
  • Or turn on Settings › Metadata › Automatically write changes into XMP sidecars.

Recover edits, history and snapshots

Writing XMP also saves the current Atelier Develop settings and masks. Develop edits mark the sidecar unsaved; automatic writing includes them. To back up edits made before this feature, select those photos and choose Library › Write XMP for Selection.

If you lose the catalog, import the originals with their sidecars into a new catalog. Atelier restores the matching current edits, saved history and snapshots before making previews, even when an import preset is selected. Photos already in the catalog keep their existing edits. RAW and JPEG files with the same name keep separate Develop entries in their shared sidecar; ratings and other standard metadata remain shared. Keep filenames unchanged for recovery.

Old metadata-only sidecars still work. Unreadable foreign metadata alone does not block import. Unsupported or damaged Atelier recovery data appears in the import problems; Atelier leaves the sidecar untouched instead of silently replacing your edits. A write that cannot safely preserve existing recovery data also reports a problem. If another, older Atelier version changes current edits while leaving saved history behind, recovery reports the mismatch instead of combining incompatible states. Keep history backups current using this version of Atelier.

New sidecar writes include retained History steps, the selected step and redo steps, plus snapshot names, dates and saved looks. Earlier sidecars containing only current edits recover one state, shown as Recovered from XMP. Write XMP explicitly to back up history from earlier edits. Atelier currently keeps up to 500 history steps per photo. Virtual copies, stacks, collections and AI provenance still require catalog backups. Subject and object masks are regenerated from their saved instructions, so edges may change with macOS. Keep separate backups of originals and external/AI result files. Other apps can read the standard metadata, but the Develop settings are specific to Atelier.

Drives and the NAS

Atelier remembers photos by drive, not by where it's mounted, so plugging a drive in somewhere else doesn't break anything. When the NAS or a drive is off, its photos stay in the grid from their previews, marked Offline. Library › Refresh Volumes (⌘R) checks again after you connect one.
